Storage

A storage unit $(u, i)$ can both inject and withdraw active power, subject to the energy it holds. What makes it its own kind of unit is not that it does both — a generator with a negative lower bound does too — but that what it can do at one network index depends on what it did at the others.

NetworkModelBuilder.AbstractStorage — Type
AbstractStorage <: AbstractUnit

A unit that can both inject and withdraw active power, subject to the energy it holds.

What makes storage its own kind of unit is not that it does both — a generator with a negative lower bound does too — but that what it can do at one network index depends on what it did at the others. Its state of charge is carried from one time step to the next, which makes it the second component, with FlexibleLoad, whose constraints span network indices.

NetworkModelBuilder.Storage — Type
Storage <: AbstractStorage

A unit (u, i) that charges and discharges within a rating and an energy capacity.

In a dispatch problem the charge and discharge power at each network index are decisions, and the state of charge follows them from step to step. In a power flow there is nothing to decide and the unit holds the setpoint p_set, q_set.

Charging and discharging are separate non-negative variables, so a round trip loses energy through both efficiencies. Nothing in the constraints forbids the two from being non-zero at once, and where a node pays to take energy off the network a unit with losses is paid to do it, by burning what it holds. exact says what is done about that, see Exclusion.

What a constructor cannot see is check_storage's: an energy held above the capacity at the first time step, a target outside the bounds at the last, a market schedule that runs the unit out. A model runs it before it builds.

Fields

  • id, name: the identifier and a human readable label.
  • node: the node the storage is connected to.
  • p_set, q_set: the injection setpoint [pu], used by a power flow, and the market schedule a RedispatchProblem moves away from.
  • e_max: the usable energy capacity [pu·h].
  • e_min: the energy the unit keeps in reserve [pu·h], the lower bound of its state of charge. 0 by default.
  • e_init: the energy held before the first time step [pu·h].
  • e_fin: the energy the unit must hold after the last time step [pu·h]. NaN, the default, means no target, and the horizon ends wherever the dispatch leaves it. It is a Pin.
  • terminal: what the end of the horizon asks, one AbstractTerminal or a list of them, none by default: Pin, Cyclic, Floor, Value, Soft and Quad. At most one end, e_fin, Pin or Cyclic, and at most one price on it, Value, Soft or Quad, which a pin has no use for.
  • p_chg_max, p_dch_max: the power limits [pu].
  • η_chg, η_dch: the one-way efficiencies, in (0, 1].
  • exact: an Exclusion, how charging and discharging at once is kept out, or a Bool, true being EXACT. RELAXED by default. EXACT and FROZEN need finite p_chg_max and p_dch_max.
  • k_cycl_max: the equivalent full cycles the unit may turn in any 24 hours [-], see fnct_stor_cycle!. A cycle counts half of the energy that goes into the store and half of the energy that comes out of it, over e_max, so that a lossless full cycle counts one. Inf, the default, leaves it free to cycle as often as the ratings allow.
  • e_cycl_init: what the unit counted in the 24 hours before the first step, as (hours, energy) per step, oldest first [h, pu·h]; empty by default. A rolling horizon fills it from the window before, see initial_state, and a problem that starts with a unit that has been cycling gives it.
  • c_chg, c_dch: the price of moving one per unit into and out of the unit [currency/pu/h], each on its own side. This is degradation, so it is paid in a dispatch and in a redispatch alike and whatever the reason the energy moved.
  • c_cycl: the price of one equivalent full cycle [currency/cycle], on the same count as k_cycl_max and charged at every network index like the two prices above, in a dispatch and in a redispatch alike.
  • q_min, q_max: the reactive power limits [pu].
  • s_max: the rating of the converter the unit sits behind [pu], positive. Inf, the default, writes no row; a unit that cannot inject is out of service, which status says. A current based formulation keeps the net injection and the reactive power inside a circle, (p^{sd} - p^{sc})^2 + q^2 <= s_max^2; a linearized one, which has no reactive power, limits the net active injection, |p^{sd} - p^{sc}| <= s_max. The charge and discharge ratings still bound each side; this bounds what the unit puts on the node.
  • c_up, c_dn: the price of injecting one per unit more and one per unit less than the market schedule, in a RedispatchProblem [currency/pu/h]. Both default to zero, which makes the unit a free measure; set them where storage is meant to be a costly one. Note that at a price of zero only the difference of the two volumes is determined — nothing then stops the solver from adding the same amount to both — so read the deviation of a free measure as psup - psdn rather than as either one. Either price may be negative, a payback, but a pair that sums below zero is refused: it would pay the solver to raise both volumes by the same amount.
  • h_cur: the curative holding time of a corrective unit [h]. The net power it takes over the contingencies, the base case included, spans at most e_max / h_cur at every time step, so that one position serves whichever of them occurs, see fnct_stor_range!. 0, the default, writes no row, and a preventive unit has the same net power in every state and none.
  • status: whether the storage is in service.
  • ext: free-form storage.

In the LPFFormulation

As above but without $q^{\text{s}}_{u}$: the charge, discharge and state of charge variables are shared between the formulations, and only the reactive power is dropped. The state of charge constraint is unchanged, for the same reason a flexible load's energy balance is.

Constraints

The injection, against the current:

\[v^{\text{r}}_{i} i^{\text{r}}_{u} + v^{\text{i}}_{i} i^{\text{i}}_{u} = p^{\text{sd}}_{u} - p^{\text{sc}}_{u}, \qquad v^{\text{i}}_{i} i^{\text{r}}_{u} - v^{\text{r}}_{i} i^{\text{i}}_{u} = q^{\text{s}}_{u} .\]

The converter limit

A unit behind a converter cannot put more on its node than the converter carries, whatever its charge and discharge ratings allow side by side. Where $s^{\text{max}}_{u}$ is finite, the IVRFormulation keeps the net injection and the reactive power inside a circle:

\[\left( p^{\text{sd}}_{u} - p^{\text{sc}}_{u} \right)^{2} + \left( q^{\text{s}}_{u} \right)^{2} \le \left( s^{\text{max}}_{u} \right)^{2} ,\]

and the LPFFormulation, which has no reactive power, limits the active power alone:

\[- s^{\text{max}}_{u} \le p^{\text{sd}}_{u} - p^{\text{sc}}_{u} \le s^{\text{max}}_{u} .\]

The default Inf writes neither, and the two power ratings are then the whole limit. A rating is positive: a unit that cannot inject is out of service, and status says so. The limit is on the net, not on each side: where the efficiencies are one the unit may charge and discharge at once, so either side can exceed $s^{\text{max}}_{u}$ as long as the difference does not. A rating that is finite at some network indices and Inf at others changes the shape of the model, see structure_gates.

In a RedispatchProblem a unit with a preventive setup holds the active power it has at the base case in every contingency, so the linearized limit is written at the base case only. The reactive power is not held, so the circle is written at every index.

Across network indices

The state of charge, carried from one time step to the next:

\[e_{u,n} = e_{u,n-1} + \Delta t_{n} \left( \eta^{\text{c}}_{u} p^{\text{sc}}_{u,n} - p^{\text{sd}}_{u,n} / \eta^{\text{d}}_{u} + p^{\text{in}}_{u,n} \right)\]

with the first step of each horizon starting from $e^{0}_{u}$. The horizon runs along :time with every other coordinate held fixed, so a problem with a contingency dimension gives each contingency its own trajectory from the same starting energy.

$p^{\text{in}}$ is inflow and is zero for a Storage. It is in the balance rather than absent from it so that a unit whose energy comes partly from outside the model — a reservoir fed by rain — overrides one method rather than the whole loop. A coupling constraint duplicated to add one term is a coupling constraint that will drift.

NetworkModelBuilder.inflow — Function
inflow(st, nm, n)

The power arriving in storage unit st from outside the model at network index n [pu], and zero for a unit that has none.

A battery is filled by the grid and by nothing else, so the balance above is complete without this. A reservoir is not: rain and rivers put energy into it that no node balance ever sees, and a subtype whose energy comes partly from outside overrides this one line rather than the whole balance. It is here rather than on the subtype for exactly that reason — a coupling constraint duplicated to add one term is a coupling constraint that will drift.

It is a power, entering the balance where the charge and discharge do and weighted by the same time step, so an inflow given as a NetworkVector over :time is a natural inflow profile.

Charging and discharging at once

Charging and discharging are two non-negative variables, and nothing in the constraints forbids both from being non-zero in the same step. With losses that is not harmless: a unit that charges and discharges $x$ at once burns $x \left( 1 / \eta^{\text{d}}_{u} - \eta^{\text{c}}_{u} \right)$ of what it holds. Where a node pays to have a unit taken off the network — a Spill that charges for spilling, a generator that pays to run — the relaxation is paid to burn energy, and a unit that is meant to hold it does not.

On three steps of a must-run surplus at a node that charges 500 to spill, a full battery with losses of ten per cent each way earns 393.00 relaxed, where 426.54 is the most it can earn and 450.00 is what the network pays without it. The relaxation is exact where the unit's own prices reach what burning is worth,

\[\eta^{\text{c}}_{u} c^{\text{d}}_{u} + c^{\text{c}}_{u} / \eta^{\text{d}}_{u} \ge \left( 1 / \eta^{\text{d}}_{u} - \eta^{\text{c}}_{u} \right) \left| \lambda \right| ,\]

with $\lambda$ the lowest price a generator or a slack unit sets at any network index, 500 there and the right hand side 105.6: a $c^{\text{d}}_{u}$ of 100 leaves the gap and one of 130 closes it. A unit that falls short of it is warned about when the model is built. A cycle limit does not settle it, since it bounds how often the unit burns and does not stop it.

exact says what is done about it, an Exclusion:

valuewhat it doesthe model afterwards
RELAXEDnothinglinear, with duals
EXACTa binary for each step that did it, then solved againmixed-integer, no duals
FROZENthe smaller side of each such step fixed to zero, then solved againlinear, with duals; feasible, not optimal

EXACT writes, for each step $n$ that the solution charged and discharged in at once, a variable $z^{\text{s}}_{u,n} \in \{0, 1\}$ and

\[p^{\text{sc}}_{u,n} \le p^{\text{sc,max}}_{u,n} \, z^{\text{s}}_{u,n} , \qquad p^{\text{sd}}_{u,n} \le p^{\text{sd,max}}_{u,n} \left( 1 - z^{\text{s}}_{u,n} \right) ,\]

and solves again until no step is left, at most eight rounds. Only the steps that need a binary get one: where the relaxation is exact there are none and the model stays a linear program. In the current based formulation the model needs a solver for nonconvex mixed-integer programs, as a free switch does.

optimize_model! does this through refine!, reports what the refined objective is above the first one as "relaxation_gap", and print_summary shows it. solve_rolling_horizon builds the window after a refined one again rather than updating it.

NetworkModelBuilder.Exclusion — Type
Exclusion

How a Storage unit is kept from charging and discharging at once, which nothing in its constraints forbids:

  • RELAXED: not at all, and no integers. This is exact where the unit's own prices cover what a node pays to take a unit off the network, η_chg c_dch + c_chg / η_dch >= (1 / η_dch - η_chg) |λ|, and a unit that falls short of it is warned about.
  • EXACT: the optimum. The model is solved, every unit-step that charges and discharges at once gets a binary z_stor and two rows, p_chg <= p_chg_max z and p_dch <= p_dch_max (1 - z), and it is solved again, until none is left. A model that did this is mixed-integer: it has no duals, and in the current based formulation it needs a solver for nonconvex mixed-integer programs.
  • FROZEN: feasible, not optimal. The direction of each such unit-step is fixed, the smaller of the two sides to zero, and the model is solved again, until none is left. It is the same kind of problem as before and keeps its duals.

A Bool is accepted where an Exclusion is, as it is for a TapMode: false is RELAXED and true is EXACT. EXACT and FROZEN need finite ratings, which what is excluded is measured against.

What is solved is a problem with losses on both sides and a price on moving energy: with losses, a unit in a step that charges and discharges at once burns what it holds, and where a node pays to have a unit taken — a Spill that charges for spilling, a generator that pays to run — the relaxation is paid to do it.

The end of the horizon

Without a condition the horizon ends wherever the dispatch leaves a unit, which is empty where the last prices are dear. What a unit can be asked instead is a list, terminal, of conditions on its energy $e_{u,n}$ after the last time step $n$, along :time with every other coordinate held fixed, so a problem with a contingency dimension asks each contingency to arrive at the same place:

conditionwhat it askswhere it is written
Pinthe energy is $e$a row
Cyclicthe energy is the one the unit started witha row, as a Pin
Floorthe energy is at least $e$a row
Valueeach unit of energy left is worth $c$the objective
Softthe energy is $e$, or the miss is paid fora row and the objective
Quadthe squared miss from $e$ is paid forthe objective

\[e_{u,n} = e \;\; (\texttt{Pin}), \qquad e_{u,n} \ge e \;\; (\texttt{Floor}), \qquad e_{u,n} - e = e^{\text{up}}_{u,n} - e^{\text{dn}}_{u,n} \;\; (\texttt{Soft}),\]

\[-c \, e_{u,n} \;\; (\texttt{Value}), \qquad c^{\uparrow} e^{\text{up}}_{u,n} + c^{\downarrow} e^{\text{dn}}_{u,n} \;\; (\texttt{Soft}), \qquad w \left( e_{u,n} - e \right)^{2} \;\; (\texttt{Quad}).\]

A target $e$ is a number or a profile over the network index, read at the last time step, and scheduled_energy is one: a redispatch that is asked to leave a unit where the market schedule leaves it ends on Pin(scheduled_energy(data, u)). e_fin is a Pin and stays as it was. A Cyclic end is a Pin on e_init, taken when the unit is made so that a window of a roll, which starts from another energy, does not move it. A unit has at most one end, e_fin, Pin or Cyclic, and at most one price on it, Value, Soft or Quad; a price on a pinned energy has nothing to price. A pin is a pin and not a floor: a target the ratings cannot reach makes the problem infeasible, which is the honest answer to a question that has none. A Floor costs nothing below what the unit would hold anyway. A condition that is not given writes nothing.

On the three steps with prices 10, 100 and 50 that a unit starting on 0.2 pu·h of its 0.5 sells in the last two, the free unit ends empty at a cost of 4.0. A Pin, a Cyclic end or a Floor of 0.2 holds it to 0.2 for 14.0, a Floor of 0.1 for 9.0. A Value of 40 changes nothing and one of 60 keeps the 0.2 and charges 0.2 more at the price of 50, since it is worth more than it costs. A Soft end with a price of 30 below its target of 0.2 lets the unit sell it, at 10.0 with the price in it, and one of 80 does not, at 14.0.

NetworkModelBuilder.AbstractTerminal — Type
AbstractTerminal

A condition on the energy a Storage unit holds after the last time step.

Without one the horizon ends wherever the dispatch leaves the unit, which is empty where the last prices are dear and a roll that cannot see past its window spends the energy before a peak. The conditions are what the end of a horizon can ask:

conditionwhat it askswhere it is written
Pinthe energy is ea row
Cyclicthe energy is what the unit started witha row, as a Pin
Floorthe energy is at least ea row
Valueeach unit of energy left is worth cthe objective
Softthe energy is e, or the miss is paid fora row and the objective

A Pin, a Cyclic end and a Floor are about the end of the problem. A price on the end, Value, Soft or Quad, is about the end of every window of a rolling horizon, which is what stops the drain; see interior_state.

A target e is a number or a profile over the network index, read at the last time step: scheduled_energy is one. A subtype adds what it needs of three methods, variable_stor_terminal! for the variables it brings, fnct_stor_final_energy! for its rows and terminal_cost for its price, each of which does nothing by default.

NetworkModelBuilder.Value — Type
Value(c)

Each unit of energy left after the last time step is worth c [currency/pu·h], taken off the objective as -c e. The unit then sells what it has where the last price is above c and holds it, and charges more, where it is below.

It is a price put on the end to shape a decision and not a cost anyone pays, so a rolling horizon leaves it out of what it reports and a problem solved whole counts it in its "objective".

NetworkModelBuilder.interior_state — Function
interior_state(component)

component as a window that does not reach the end of the horizon should see it, with every end-of-horizon target released.

The counterpart of initial_state, and the pair is what makes a rolling horizon ask the same question the problem it is rolling asks. initial_state carries the start of a window forward from the one before it; this holds the end back, because a window's last time step is the horizon's last time step only for the window that closes it.

Returned unchanged unless the type says otherwise. A Storage unit with an e_fin has a method; nothing else in the package aims at anything, so nothing else needs one.

source
interior_state(st)

The storage unit st with what belongs to the end of the problem released: e_fin, a Pin, a Cyclic end and a Floor.

The counterpart of initial_state, and the two are what make a rolling horizon mean the same thing as the problem it is rolling. One carries the start of a window forward from the window before it; this holds the end back, for a window whose last time step is not the last time step of the problem.

Without it every window would be asked to arrive at e_fin, which is EnergyGuide-style stitching by another name: a target invented for each batch because the batches do not talk to each other. They do talk here, through initial_state, so only the window that actually reaches the end of the horizon carries the target.

A price on the end, Value, Soft or Quad, stays. It is not a target the batches have to agree on but what the energy left at the end of a window is worth, and a window that cannot see past its end needs exactly that to know not to spend it.

source
What a rolling horizon does with it

The last time step of a window is the last time step of the problem only for the window that closes it, so a roll releases a Pin, a Cyclic end and a Floor everywhere else, see interior_state. Asking every window to arrive at one is stitching batches together by pinning both their ends to a guessed profile, which is what initial_state exists so as not to need.

The trade is real: a lookahead too short to see why the energy is being kept will spend it. Eight steps with ten five times and a hundred three times, a unit that charges at ten and sells at a hundred, and a roll with no condition pay 26.2, 10.1 and 8.4 above perfect foresight at horizons 2, 3 and 4, and nothing at 5. A price on the end stays in every window, which is what it is for. Below what the peak pays after the losses it does not hold the unit back, and a Value of 90 here costs nothing at any of the four. Above it the unit keeps its energy to the end, and the roll pays for the steps it did not sell.

A price on the end is not a cost

It shapes a decision and nobody pays it, so it is in the "objective" of a problem solved whole and not in what a rolling horizon reports, which is the cost of the steps it commits and of the periods it closes. A unit that keeps its energy at the end of a roll for a value is not credited for it. See terminal_cost.

Out of service

A storage unit is taken out of service with its status, which may vary over :time or over :contingency, and not with a rating of zero. A unit out of service at a network index has no variables, no rows and no entry in the solution there, and it holds the energy it had at the last step it was in service, with no self-discharge:

\[e_{u,n} = e_{u,n-1}, \qquad e_{u,n} = e^{0}_{u} \;\; \text{before the first step it is in service}\]

energy_at reads that. The balance of the step after a gap starts from it, and so do the conditions on the end of the horizon, which are asked of the energy the unit holds at the last step whether it is in service there or not: a Pin on a unit that is out at the last step is a pin on what it left. A unit that is out for the whole of a trajectory, as when a contingency loses it, has no state of charge there and is asked nothing. A step a unit is out of service at counts nothing toward its cycle limit. A rolling horizon carries the energy a unit holds at the last step a window commits, in service or not, so that a unit that is out when a window ends is not refilled by the next.

On five steps with prices 10, 10, 100, 10 and 100, a unit that is out at steps 2 and 3 costs 28.216 against 10.432 in service, which is what ratings of zero over the two steps cost. A preventive unit that is lost in a contingency is refused by a redispatch, since the setting it takes in the base case is one it cannot take there; a redispatch that loses a storage unit poses the unit corrective, with an exception of the Redispatch setup.

energy_at(nm, u, n)

The cycle limit

A unit that carries a finite k_cycl_max gets a row at every network index, over the time steps up to it that fit in 24 hours:

\[\sum_{m \in \mathcal{W}_{n}} \frac{\Delta t_{m}}{2} \left( \eta^{\text{c}}_{u} p^{\text{sc}}_{u,m} + p^{\text{sd}}_{u,m} / \eta^{\text{d}}_{u} \right) \le k^{\text{max}}_{u} \, e^{\text{max}}_{u,n} - \hat{e}_{u,n}\]

with the window $\mathcal{W}_{n}$ running along :time back from $n$, $n$ included, for as long as the steps fit in 24 hours, and every other coordinate held fixed. $\hat{e}_{u,n}$ is what e_cycl_init counted of the hours that reach back before the first step, which is nothing unless it was given or a rolling horizon filled it. The limit holds over any 24 hours and not over a calendar day, which a unit could turn twice over across midnight, so a grouping of :time into periods does not apply to it.

The right hand side is the energy the unit may count, k_cycl_max capacities, and a step counts half of what goes into the store and half of what comes out of it: one capacity charged and discharged counts one cycle, in a lossless unit and in a lossy one. A step the unit is out of service at counts nothing. In a redispatch a preventive unit holds its schedule across the contingencies and has the rows of the base case only; a corrective one has them in every contingency, see is_held.

An infinite limit — the default — is the absence of a row rather than a row with an infinite right hand side. A right hand side that the energy already counted has used up is taken as zero.

NetworkModelBuilder.fnct_stor_cycle! — Function
fnct_stor_cycle!(nm, T)

Hold every storage unit that carries a cycle limit to it over any 24 hours, with a row at every network index in service,

\[\sum_{m \in \mathcal{W}_{n}} \frac{\Delta t_{m}}{2} \left( \eta^{\text{c}}_{u} p^{\text{sc}}_{u,m} + p^{\text{sd}}_{u,m} / \eta^{\text{d}}_{u} \right) \le k^{\text{max}}_{u} \, e^{\text{max}}_{u,n} - \hat{e}_{u,n} ,\]

with the window 𝒲 the time steps up to n, n included, that fit in 24 hours, along :time and with every other coordinate held fixed, and ê what e_cycl_init counted of the 24 hours that reach back before the first step. A cycle is counted on both sides of the store, half of what goes into it and half of what comes out of it, over the capacity, so a lossless full cycle counts one and charging and discharging in the same step spends the allowance twice. A limit over any 24 hours and not over a calendar day is what a warranty says, and it cannot be turned twice over across midnight.

A step the unit is out of service at counts nothing. A unit that is held to the base case in a redispatch, see is_held, has the rows of the base case only, which imply the others; a corrective one has them in every state. A limit of infinity is not a row with an infinite right hand side; it is the absence of a row, and a solver handed the first would be given a constraint it can never use. A right hand side that the energy already counted has used up is taken as zero.

Why both sides

A unit that discharges what it holds draws k_cycl_max · e_max / η_dch out of its store, more than the limit says it delivers, and the same unit charging draws it from the grid before the loss. Counting both sides, half of each, is the one count that sees all of that and keeps a lossless cycle at one. It is also what makes charging and discharging in the same step, which the relaxation allows, spend the allowance twice. Charging and discharging are priced apart for the same reason.

A roll carries what it has counted

solve_rolling_horizon hands the trailing 24 hours of the count from one window to the next through initial_state, so that a unit does not get its allowance back at every window. A full unit with a limit of 0.1 pu·h that sells at 100 in the first of four steps sells 0.2 in all, rolled two steps at a time as well as whole, and not 0.2 again in the next window. Independent problems, such as the chunks of a pipeline run in separate processes, start with nothing counted unless e_cycl_init is given.

A cycle limit does not stop charging and discharging at once

Where a node pays to have energy taken off the network, a unit with losses is paid to burn what it holds by charging and discharging at once. A cycle limit bounds how often it does so and does not stop it; see Charging and discharging at once.

The curative range

A corrective unit is free to take another position in every contingency, which a unit that takes time to move is not: one position taken in advance has to serve whichever contingency occurs. A positive h_cur, the hours the unit takes to move across, holds its net power over the contingencies in one band at every time step,

\[p^{\text{min}}_{u,n} \le p^{\text{sd}}_{u,m} - p^{\text{sc}}_{u,m} \le p^{\text{max}}_{u,n} \quad \forall m \in \mathcal{C}_{n}, \qquad p^{\text{max}}_{u,n} - p^{\text{min}}_{u,n} \le e^{\text{max}}_{u,n} / h^{\text{cur}}_{u} ,\]

with $\mathcal{C}_{n}$ the :contingency coordinates of the time step the unit is in service at, the base case among them. The band is two free variables per time step, and the unit may centre it by moving its output in the base case; its trajectory in each state stays its own. A preventive unit has the same net power in every state and is given no row, nor is a unit with no h_cur, a problem with a single state or an optimal power flow. e_min is another thing, a floor on the energy in every state.

On one step and three contingencies that need 0, 0.2 and 0.6 of relief, a free unit of 0.5 pu·h behind the constraint gives 0, 0.2 and 0.5 for an expected cost of 6.0. At an h_cur of 2 h its band is 0.25 wide and it gives 0.25, 0.25 and 0.5, at 7.0, since what it gives in the first two states comes off the generator at the far end whether it is needed or not; at an e_max of 0.8 the band is 0.4 and it gives 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5, at 6.3333. At an h_cur of 1 h the band is as wide as the states are apart, and the cost is 6.0 again.

NetworkModelBuilder.fnct_stor_range! — Function
fnct_stor_range!(nm, T)

Hold the net power of every corrective storage unit that carries a curative holding time in one band over the states of the world, at every time step,

\[p^{\text{min}}_{u,n} \le p^{\text{sd}}_{u,m} - p^{\text{sc}}_{u,m} \le p^{\text{max}}_{u,n} \quad \forall m \in \mathcal{C}_{n}, \qquad p^{\text{max}}_{u,n} - p^{\text{min}}_{u,n} \le e^{\text{max}}_{u,n} / h^{\text{cur}}_{u} ,\]

Before the build

A constructor refuses what is wrong with a unit on its own: a negative rating, an energy floor above the capacity, a price that is negative or not finite, a pair c_up, c_dn that sums below zero. What needs the dimension is checked before a model builds, at the network index the energy is read at: the start against the bounds at the first time step, the target against them at the last, and, in a redispatch, whether the market schedule p_set can be followed at all.

A schedule that discharges more than the unit holds is not infeasible to a redispatch. It moves the unit away from it at the price of its volumes, and the bill shows as redispatch cost with no congestion behind it, which is why it is refused instead.

NetworkModelBuilder.check_storage — Function
check_storage(data; dispatch = true, schedule = true)

Refuse storage data that cannot pose a sensible problem, with an ArgumentError that names the unit and the network index; instantiate_model calls it before it builds. What a constructor cannot see is read here, at the network index the energy is read at:

  • with dispatch, an e_init outside [e_min, e_max] at the first time step and an e_fin outside it at the last, each against the bounds of that index, and so is a target of a Pin, Soft or Quad, or a Floor above the capacity or above the pin. A power flow holds a setpoint and has no state of charge to bound.
  • with schedule, a market schedule p_set that leaves the energy outside the bounds, see scheduled_energy, or that counts more than the cycle limit allows over any 24 hours. A redispatch repairs such a schedule at the price of its volumes and books the bill as redispatch, with no congestion behind it.

A cycle counts half of what goes into the store and what comes out of it, Δt (η_chg max(-p_set, 0) + max(p_set, 0)/η_dch) / 2 over e_max, so that a lossless full cycle counts one. The budget is k_cycl_max · e_max over any run of time steps that spans 24 hours or less, not over a calendar day.

Nothing is checked without a :time dimension: nothing then couples, and a dispatch problem asks for one when it builds. An energy is allowed 1.0e-6 outside a bound, since what a solver hands to the next window carries digits.

NetworkModelBuilder.scheduled_energy — Function
scheduled_energy(data, u)

The energy storage unit u holds after every network index when it follows its market schedule p_set and nothing else, as a NetworkVector.

A positive p_set is a discharge and a negative one a charge, so the energy moves by

\[\Delta t_{n} \left( \eta^{\text{c}}_{u} \max(-p^{\text{s}}_{u,n}, 0) - \max(p^{\text{s}}_{u,n}, 0) / \eta^{\text{d}}_{u} \right)\]

from e_init at the first step of each horizon, along :time with every other coordinate held fixed. A unit out of service holds what it has. An inflow, which a subtype brings from outside the model, is not counted, and the helper is for a Storage for that reason.

Cost

whatcode namewhere it is paid
moving energyc_chg, c_dchdispatch_cost, once per network index
turning a cyclec_cycldispatch_cost and redispatch_cost, once per network index
leaving a market schedulec_up, c_dnredispatch_cost, once per network index

The first two measure different wear and a unit may carry either or both: a battery that discharges half its capacity twice pays for one cycle, while the throughput price charges it for the whole of what it moved either way. All three are also in redispatch_cost, because degradation does not care why the energy moved — a battery asked to relieve a congestion wears exactly as much as one following a schedule, and c_up and c_dn price the deviation rather than the movement.

It needs a time dimension

Like a FlexibleLoad, a storage unit couples network indices along :time and says so when the problem has no such dimension.

What a battery is not here

A battery is a constant efficiency on each side of one energy balance, priced by c_chg, c_dch and c_cycl. A loss that depends on the power, a rate capacity at short time steps and a degradation map convexified in power and energy are what Fortenbacher et al. (2017) put inside the model, and a self-discharge rate is not here either.

  • Fortenbacher, P.; Mathieu, J. L. and Andersson, G. (2017). Modeling and Optimal Operation of Distributed Battery Storage in Low Voltage Grids. IEEE Transactions on Power Systems 32, 4340–4350, arXiv:1603.06468.
