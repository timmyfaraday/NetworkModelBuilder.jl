Nomenclature

Every quantity the package names follows one grammar: the fields of a component, the keys of its variables, the keys of a solution and the keywords that carry a quantity. A name is a symbol, the letter of the quantity, followed by at most two descriptors and, where the quantity is not in per unit, a unit:

symbol _ primary _ secondary _ unit

v_magn_max is the upper limit of a voltage magnitude, i_term_real the real part of a terminal current, p_chg_max the charge power limit of a storage unit and v_base_kv the voltage base in kV. Each name reads as a short sentence, and a name that reads the same in the code and in the docs needs no translation.

The rules

  • Lowercase ASCII, snake_case and singular. The one exception is the symbol η of an efficiency.
  • A descriptor has two to four letters and is taken from the lists below.
  • The descriptors come in a fixed order: a what, then a part, then a qualifier, at most two of them. The first is the primary, the second the secondary descriptor. An empty slot is dropped: v_magn_max has a part and a qualifier, i_ser_real has a what and a part.
  • A unit comes last, and only where the quantity is not in per unit or in radians.
  • What is not a quantity stays a plain word.

An index letter is never a quantity, so a symbol may share its letter with one: i_term_real is a current, while the i of $i \in I$ is a node.

The descriptors

A what says where a quantity is, or whose it is:

descriptormeaning
terma terminal of an edge
serthe series element of a branch
shntthe shunt element of an edge
mgntthe magnetising branch of a transformer
starthe star point of a transformer
pntthe point of a branch with more than two terminals
windbehind the ratio of a winding
gen, stor, flex, slk, unita generator, a storage unit, a flexible load, a slack unit, any unit
dcl, swa DC link, a switch

loss, base, ovl, cycl, per, cura loss, a base, an overload, a cycle, a period, a curative one

A part says which part of a complex or polar quantity: real, imag, magn for the magnitude and angl for the angle. Where nothing says what, the part is the primary descriptor: v_real, v_angl.

A qualifier says what kind of number it is:

descriptormeaning
min, maxa lower and an upper bound
set, init, fin, nom, mkta setpoint, an initial value, a final value, a nominal value, the market schedule
up, dnupward and downward
fix, prop, thru, peak, step, enerfixed, proportional, throughput, peak, step size, per unit of energy

A unit is kv, mva or km.

Words are not quantities and are not built from the grammar: id, name, node, terminals, status, ext, type, area, zone, lock, position, reverse, oltc, pst and exact.

Fields and keys

A field and a key follow the same grammar and differ in one respect. A key names its owner, because the keys of every component of a network index share one dictionary: :p_gen, :p_stor_up, :p_term. A role that only one owner has says it instead: :p_chg and :p_dch are a storage unit's. A field does not name its owner, the type already says whose it is:

using NetworkModelBuilder

(generator = fieldnames(Generator), storage = fieldnames(Storage))
(generator = (:id, :name, :node, :p_set, :q_set, :p_min, :p_max, :q_min, :q_max, :v_magn_set, :e_per_max, :e_per_init, :c, :c_up, :c_dn, :status, :ext), storage = (:id, :name, :node, :p_set, :q_set, :e_max, :e_min, :e_init, :e_fin, :terminal, :p_chg_max, :p_dch_max, :η_chg, :η_dch, :exact, :k_cycl_max, :e_cycl_init, :c_chg, :c_dch, :c_cycl, :q_min, :q_max, :s_max, :c_up, :c_dn, :h_cur, :status, :ext))

Generator.p_max and Storage.p_chg_max are both an active power limit. A solution key is the key of the variable it reports, "p_gen" for :p_gen, and a parameter that is given back takes its field name, "g" and "b" of a shunt.

Two fields deserve a note, because their names say less than they mean:

  • v_angl_min and v_angl_max of an edge limit the voltage angle difference across it, not an angle.
  • s_max bounds apparent power, which is active power wherever there is no reactive power: for a DCLink and in the linearized formulation.

The names of Matpower, PowerModels and Zorba are kept in their readers and nowhere else: the package reads the base power of a Matpower case and calls it s_base_mva.

The keys of ext that the package reads for a quantity follow the grammar too: a Node starts its voltage at ext[:v_real_init] and ext[:v_imag_init], or its angle at ext[:v_angl_init]. The other keys of ext are free-form.

In the mathematics

The code name column of the tables on the component pages joins a name in the code to the symbol in the formulas. The formulas use the same letters: $v$ for voltage, $i$ for current, $p$ for power, $c$ for cost, $\eta$ for efficiency, with a subscript for an index and a superscript for a descriptor, see Notation. A letter that is an index keeps its meaning: $i$ is also a node, as in $v^{\text{r}}_{i}$, and $i^{\text{r}}_{u}$ is the current a unit injects.

Constraints, functions and duals

A constraint, the function that writes it and the dual it gives share one stem, owner_role, and take one prefix each, which says which of the three a name is:

kindnamefor the node balance
the constraintcstr_ and the stemcstr_node_balance
the function that writes itfnct_ and the stemfnct_node_balance
its dualdual_ and the stem, then the part it pricesdual_node_balance_real

The owner is whoever writes the row: a family, node, edge or unit, where several types write it; a type, br for a branch, tf for a transformer, dcl, sw, gen, stor, flex or shnt; or the problem, rd for a redispatch. The role is what the row states, one or two words from a closed list: balance, voltage_reference, angle_difference, final_energy, tap_setting. A key never names the formulation, as :p_term does not: cstr_edge_rating is the rating of an edge in the current based and in the linearized formulation. A function written for the linearized formulation alone keeps that word, as fnct_edge_rating_linear! does, and those that walk a registry and write the rows of every type of a family take the family as the stem: fnct_edge and fnct_unit.

A row is registered under its key and an id: the identifier of its component, or a tuple of it and its parts, (i, :real), (e, :fr, :imag), (a, :magn, :max). A part is real, imag, magn or angl; a bound, min or max; a side, fr or to; ser for the series element and set for a setpoint; or one of loss, forward, reverse, pos, neg, side, p, q, chg and dch. The current based formulation writes a row for each part where the linearized one writes one, so an id has one shape per key within a formulation, not across the two.

In debug mode a row is named from its network index, its key and its id, a tuple flattened, which is what a solver log or an LP file shows:

using JuMP

nm = instantiate_model("case14.m", OptimalPowerFlowProblem, IVRFormulation; debug_mode = true)

rows = registered_constraints(nm)
(keys   = sort!(unique(String(key) for (_, key, _) in keys(rows))),
 names  = sort!([JuMP.name(first(row)) for ((_, key, _), row) in rows if key === :cstr_node_balance])[1:4])
(keys = ["cstr_br_pi_section", "cstr_node_balance", "cstr_node_voltage_limit", "cstr_node_voltage_reference", "cstr_shnt_current", "cstr_tf_star_balance", "cstr_tf_winding", "cstr_unit_power"], names = ["1_cstr_node_balance[1,imag]", "1_cstr_node_balance[1,real]", "1_cstr_node_balance[10,imag]", "1_cstr_node_balance[10,real]"])

The duals a node reports are named for the balance they price. dual_node_balance_p and dual_node_balance_q are the price of active and of reactive power, and in the current based formulation dual_node_balance_real and dual_node_balance_imag are the prices of a unit of current they are recovered from, see nodal_prices.

What is not covered

The functions of the families variable_*, solution_* and objective_*, and the plain words, are not part of the grammar. "dual_status" is JuMP's own word, the status of the solve, not a dual.

test/nomenclature.jl holds the grammar as code and checks every field, every variable key, every solution key, every constraint key and id, every function that writes rows and every dual of the package against it; test/docs_tables.jl checks that the tables of the component pages list exactly the fields and the variable keys that exist.
