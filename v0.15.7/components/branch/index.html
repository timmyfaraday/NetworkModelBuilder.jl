Branch

A branch is an edge that transports power between its terminals without transforming it. With two terminals it is the π-equivalent $(e, i, j)$ with no turns ratio; with more it is one π-section from each terminal to a common point. Where an edge does have a ratio it is a Transformer, not a branch.

NetworkModelBuilder.AbstractBranch — Type
AbstractBranch <: AbstractEdge

An edge that transports electrical power between its terminals without transforming it: a π-equivalent between two of them, and one π-section from each terminal to a common point where there are more.

Every branch type shares the same equations. A cable and an overhead line are not types of their own: in a steady-state model both are a series impedance between two shunt admittances, so each is a Branch with its own data.

A concrete branch carries id, name, terminals (two or more), r, x, g_shnt, b_shnt, s_max, v_angl_min, v_angl_max, l_km, status and ext. Every field but id, name, terminals, l_km and ext may be a NetworkVector.

NetworkModelBuilder.Branch — Type
Branch <: AbstractBranch

An edge of two or more terminals, modelled as π-sections.

With two terminals it is the π-equivalent (e, i, j): a series impedance between two shunt admittances. With more it is one π-section from each terminal to a common point, the shunts at the point aggregated, as a line with a tee is. The point is not a node: it has no identifier and appears in neither ids(net, Node) nor the node part of a solution, it is a pair of variables belonging to the edge.

This is what a Matpower branch without a turns ratio becomes, and what a cable or an overhead line is: they differ in l_km and in a rating that may follow the weather, not in the equations. Where the edge has a turns ratio it is a Transformer, not a branch.

Fields

Every field with an entry per terminal is indexed by terminal position, and entry k belongs to the section that starts at terminal k.

  • id, name: the identifier and a human readable label.
  • terminals: the nodes the sections start at, two or more.
  • r, x: the series resistance and reactance of each section [pu].
  • g_shnt, b_shnt: the shunt admittance of each section [pu], half of it at each end of the section. A line with total charging susceptance b has b_shnt = b. On a Transformer the same names are the shunt at the terminal side of a winding.
  • s_max: the apparent power rating at each terminal [pu], positive, Inf when unlimited. A branch that cannot carry is out of service, which status says.
  • v_angl_min, v_angl_max: the limits on the voltage angle difference between the two terminals of a branch with two [rad]. A branch with more has none.
  • l_km: the route length [km], NaN where it is not known.
  • status: whether the branch is in service.
  • ext: free-form storage.

Two terminals are one section: a scalar stands for the first entry of r, x, g_shnt and b_shnt, whose second entry is 0 and has to be, and a scalar s_max for every terminal. With more terminals each of them is a vector, but s_max, which may still be one number.

A branch is refused when an entry of r, x, g_shnt or b_shnt is not finite, when a section has no impedance, r and x both 0, when s_max is not positive, when its angle limits are out of order, or when its l_km is negative or infinite. A bus coupler has no impedance to give: it is a Switch.

source

There is one branch type. A cable and an overhead line are a Branch with another l_km and another rating, not types of their own: what separates them in a steady-state model is the data they carry, not the physics — see Where a type earns its place.

Two terminals are one section. A scalar stands for the first entry of r, x, g_shnt and b_shnt, and the second entry is 0 — and has to be: a branch whose second section carries something has three terminals. A scalar s_max is the rating of every terminal. With more terminals each of them is a vector, but s_max, which may still be one number.

A line whose total charging susceptance is $b$ has $b^{\text{sh}} = b$, which is how a Matpower branch is read; half of it sits at each end. Every field but id, name, terminals, l_km and ext may be a NetworkVector; an outage in a contingency is a status that varies, and a rating that follows the weather is a s_max that does.

On a Transformer the names g_shnt and b_shnt are the shunt at the terminal side of a winding, once; on a branch they are the whole shunt of a section, half at each end.

A branch is refused when it cannot be modelled: an entry of r, x, g_shnt or b_shnt that is not finite, a section with r and x both 0 (a bus coupler is a Switch), a rating that is negative or NaN, v_angl_min above v_angl_max, or a l_km that is negative or infinite.

The π-equivalent

A branch with two terminals has one section. With $y^{\text{sh}}_{e} = g^{\text{sh}}_{e,1} + j b^{\text{sh}}_{e,1}$ its shunt admittance, half of it at each end, $z_e = r_{e,1} + j x_{e,1}$ the series impedance, $a^{\text{f}}$ and $a^{\text{t}}$ the two arcs of the edge and $i^{\text{s}}_{e}$ the series current:

\[\begin{aligned} i_{a^{\text{f}}} &= \tfrac{1}{2} y^{\text{sh}}_{e} \, v_{i} + i^{\text{s}}_{e}, \\ i_{a^{\text{t}}} &= -i^{\text{s}}_{e} + \tfrac{1}{2} y^{\text{sh}}_{e} \, v_{j}, \\ v_{i} - v_{j} &= z_{e} \, i^{\text{s}}_{e}. \end{aligned}\]

The first two split the terminal current over the shunt and the series branch; the third is the drop across the series impedance. A Transformer without a magnetising branch writes the same three equations with the from side behind an ideal ratio.

More than two terminals

A branch with $n$ terminals is $n$ of these sections, section $k$ running from terminal $k$ to a point $p$ the sections share. The half of the shunt of a section that is at its far end is at the point, where the shunts of all of them add up. With $i^{\text{s}}_{k}$ the current in section $k$, that is the terminal current less the half of the shunt at the terminal:

\[\begin{aligned} i^{\text{s}}_{k} &= i_{a_k} - \tfrac{1}{2} y^{\text{sh}}_{e,k} \, v_{i_k}, \\ v_{i_k} - v^{\text{p}}_{e} &= z_{e,k} \, i^{\text{s}}_{k}, \\ \sum_{k} i^{\text{s}}_{k} &= \tfrac{1}{2} \Big( \sum_{k} y^{\text{sh}}_{e,k} \Big) v^{\text{p}}_{e}. \end{aligned}\]

The point is a pair of variables of the edge, not a node: it has no identifier and appears in neither ids(net, Node) nor the node part of a solution. With two terminals there is no point: the one section runs from the first terminal to the second, which is the π-equivalent above.

This is the model of a Transformer with every ratio one, with $y^{\text{sh}}_{e,k}/2$ as the shunt behind each winding and $\sum_k y^{\text{sh}}_{e,k}/2$ as its magnetising branch; the two are written independently and test/multiterminal.jl checks each against the other.

Rating and angle difference

A dispatch problem adds, per terminal,

\[\left((v^{\text{r}}_{i})^2 + (v^{\text{i}}_{i})^2\right) \left((i^{\text{r}}_{a})^2 + (i^{\text{i}}_{a})^2\right) \le (s^{\text{max}}_{e,k})^2 ,\]

which is the apparent power at that terminal against the rating, and for a branch with two terminals

\[\tan(\theta^{\text{min}}_{e}) \left(v^{\text{r}}_{i} v^{\text{r}}_{j} + v^{\text{i}}_{i} v^{\text{i}}_{j}\right) \le v^{\text{i}}_{i} v^{\text{r}}_{j} - v^{\text{r}}_{i} v^{\text{i}}_{j} \le \tan(\theta^{\text{max}}_{e}) \left(v^{\text{r}}_{i} v^{\text{r}}_{j} + v^{\text{i}}_{i} v^{\text{i}}_{j}\right) ,\]

which bounds the angle difference across the edge. A branch with more terminals has no angle limit, as a transformer with more windings has none. Both are skipped where the data leaves them unbounded.

In the LPFFormulation

With two terminals,

\[p_{a^{\text{f}}} = -b_{e} \left(v^{\text{a}}_{i} - v^{\text{a}}_{j}\right), \qquad p_{a^{\text{t}}} = -p_{a^{\text{f}}} ,\]

with $b_{e}$ the series susceptance, see susceptance. With more, each section is a flow to the angle $v^{\text{ap}}_{e}$ of the point, and the flows balance there:

\[p_{a_k} = -b_{e,k} \left(v^{\text{a}}_{i_k} - v^{\text{ap}}_{e}\right), \qquad \sum_{k} p_{a_k} = 0 .\]

The shunt admittance plays no part. The rating of every terminal becomes a bound, $-s^{\text{max}}_{e,k} \le p_{a_k} \le s^{\text{max}}_{e,k}$, written by fnct_edge_rating_linear!, and the angle difference limit of a branch with two terminals, written by fnct_edge_angle_difference_linear!, is linear already. The two are separate rows: a rating and an angle limit are not one constraint.

NetworkModelBuilder.susceptance — Function
susceptance(r, x)

The series susceptance of an impedance z = r + jx, i.e., the imaginary part of 1/z:

\[b = \frac{-x}{r^2 + x^2} .\]

The resistance is kept. Dropping it as well, b = -1/x, is the other common convention and gives Matpower's DC model; the three approximations this package makes — unit voltage magnitude, no reactive power, small angles — do not require it.

NetworkModelBuilder.fnct_edge_flow_linear! — Function
fnct_edge_flow_linear!(nm, e, a_fr, a_to, b, shift; nw)

Add the linearized flow of a two-terminal edge,

\[p_{a^{\text{f}}} = -b_{e} \left(v^{\text{a}}_{i} - v^{\text{a}}_{j} - t^{\text{a}}_{e}\right), \qquad p_{a^{\text{t}}} = -p_{a^{\text{f}}} ,\]

where shift is the phase shift the edge applies, zero for a branch. The second equation is what makes the model lossless: whatever leaves one terminal arrives at the other.

shift may be a number or a variable, which is what lets a winding of a Transformer that is a pst be a control here.

NetworkModelBuilder.fnct_edge_rating_linear! — Function
fnct_edge_rating_linear!(nm, e, arcs, s_max; nw)

The rating at each terminal of an edge with any number of them in a linearized formulation: a bound on the terminal power, since with no reactive power in the model apparent power is active power. s_max is one number for every terminal or one per terminal, and a terminal whose rating is not finite has no row. Skipped where no terminal has a finite rating, and where the problem does not watch the edge for congestion, see is_monitored.

\[-s^{\text{max}}_{a} \le p_{a} \le s^{\text{max}}_{a} .\]

Where the problem prices congestion rather than forbidding it, see overload_price, the rating is relaxed by the overload of the edge and written as a pair of one-sided rows per terminal,

\[p_{a} - o_{e} \le s^{\text{max}}_{a}, \qquad -p_{a} - o_{e} \le s^{\text{max}}_{a},\]

rather than as one two-sided bound: the bounds of a range constraint are constants, and o_e is not. The pair is what a bound on |p_a| becomes once the allowance is a variable, and it is exact without a binary because the objective pays for o_e and so never lifts it further than one of the two rows requires.

fnct_edge_angle_difference_linear!(nm, a_fr, a_to, v_angl_min, v_angl_max; nw)

fnct_br_pi_section!(nm, i_wind_real, i_wind_imag, v_wind_real, v_wind_imag, a_to, e, z, y_fr, y_to; nw)

NetworkModelBuilder.fnct_edge_rating! — Function
fnct_edge_rating!(nm, e, s_max; nw)

Bound the apparent power at every terminal of edge e by s_max, (v^{\text{r}}_i{}^2 + v^{\text{i}}_i{}^2)(i^{\text{r}}_a{}^2 + i^{\text{i}}_a{}^2) \le (s^{\text{max}}_e)^2. Written per terminal, it applies to an edge with any number of them. s_max is one number for every terminal or one per terminal, and a terminal whose rating is not finite is not bounded.

Skipped where the data leaves every rating unbounded, and where the problem does not watch the edge for congestion, see is_monitored.

Where the problem prices congestion rather than forbidding it, see overload_price, the bound is relaxed by the overload of the edge,

\[(v^{\text{r}}_i{}^2 + v^{\text{i}}_i{}^2)(i^{\text{r}}_a{}^2 + i^{\text{i}}_a{}^2) \le (s^{\text{max}}_e + o_e)^2 ,\]

with one o_e ≥ 0 shared by every terminal, so the overload is the excess of the worst of them rather than a separate allowance for each. The constraint stays the same degree it already was and is written under its own key, since a row whose set is ≤ (s^{max})^2 cannot be updated in place into one holding a variable.

NetworkModelBuilder.variable_edge_series_current — Function
variable_edge_series_current(nm, E; nw)
variable_edge_series_current(nm, T; nw)

The series current of every edge in E, or of every in-service edge of type T, added to the :i_ser_real and :i_ser_imag containers shared by every edge type that has one.

The container is a Dict rather than a JuMP array because several edge types contribute to it: the dispatcher visits one concrete type at a time, and each adds its own edges to the same two containers.
