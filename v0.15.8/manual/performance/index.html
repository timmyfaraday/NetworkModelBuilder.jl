Performance history

How long a rolling-horizon redispatch takes has changed a good deal between versions. This page records, version by version, what was changed to make it faster and what each change was measured to do. The figures are measurements on one machine (112 cores, 1 TB of memory) and say how large a change was there, not what it will be elsewhere.

The workload

Every figure comes from the three-step redispatch pipeline in scripts/run_year_redispatch.jl, run over one year of real network data: 8,760 hours, 234 nodes and 399 branches, with 13 cross-border and 79 internal contingencies.

  1. A load flow at the market-cleared dispatch.
  2. A cross-border N-1 redispatch, in rolling windows of 8 hours that commit 8.
  3. An internal N-1 redispatch, in rolling windows of 1 hour that commit 1. One window is 80 network indices, the base case and 79 contingencies, with about 249,000 variables and 274,500 constraints.

No hour depends on another there (storage is left out, nothing ramps), so the year is cut into 365 chunks of 24 hours that solve independently. A chunk runs all three steps for its hours. The year is run as 73 one-thread processes of five chunks each, all at once, with two solver threads per solve. A chunk's time is what the tables below call chunk time; the wall time of a year is that of its slowest process.

From 0.12.0 to 0.12.3 the wall time fell by 54 % and the chunk time by 57 %; from 0.12.3 to 0.15.7 by a further 28.5 % and 25.3 %. A year of 0.15.7 takes a third of the time of one of 0.12.0.

The three runs solve the same problem. Objectives agree between 0.12.0 and 0.12.3 to 2.6e-8 relative (median 1e-14). Between 0.12.3 and 0.15.7 every objective, every overload row and every unit-hour volume is identical. In all three years no chunk needed the fallback solver and none had a solution that broke one of its own constraints by more than 1e-3.

How the effects were measured

  • A pair of weeks. A change is run over hours 1 to 168, seven chunks, as seven one-thread processes beside seven of the version before it, started together, on the same hours; where the pair was run twice the launch order was swapped. The chunk times above are from such pairs. The same code run three days apart differed by 5 %, the machine being busier, so a change is only compared with a control run the same day.
  • The year runs of the first table are one run each, days apart, without a same-day control. They agree with the single changes: those up to 0.12.3 compound to -55 % of chunk time, against the -57 % measured, and 0.12.5 and 0.12.6 alone to -22 %, against the -25 % measured, the rest being 0.12.9 and the state of the machine.
  • One window. benchmark/window.jl times one window of a rolling redispatch on case14 with an outage per branch, stage by stage, and is run beside a worktree of the other version. Against 0.12.4, 0.12.7 shows update_model! at 26.9 ms against 30.4, build_solution at 4.7 ms against 9.3 and the whole roll at 706 ms against 868, with the solve unchanged at 35.7 ms.

Where the time goes now

In a chunk of 0.15.7, 177 s on average, step 3 takes 147 s (83 %), step 2 26 s (15 %) and the load flow 4 s (2 %). The solver takes 42 s (24 %) and garbage collection 24 s (13.5 %); a chunk allocates 25 GB. The rest is Julia work around the solve: cutting windows, building and updating the model, building the solution and checking it.

Settings that did not help

  • Threads in one process. Seven chunks as seven threads of one process took 224 s a chunk; as seven processes 125 s. Garbage collection was 14.4 % of a chunk in one and 14.2 % in the other, so the collector is not what the threads lose to. Run many one-thread processes.
  • Longer windows. Windows of 48 hours committing 8 gave the same answer as 8 and 1 hour but took 4,944 s for hours 1 to 48, against about 670 s for two 24-hour chunks, and 27 GB. With no coupling between hours a longer window only adds model size and overlap.

Running a year

The header of scripts/run_year_redispatch.jl has the recipe: one-thread Julia processes, each on its own range of hours (NMB_HOURS) with a shared NMB_RUN_ID, NMB_CHUNK_HOURS = 24, NMB_XPRESS_THREADS = 2 and NMB_MERGE = 0, then one last run over the whole range with NMB_RESUME = 1, which finds every chunk done and only merges them.
